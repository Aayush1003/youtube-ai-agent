# 365-Day Interview-Ready Challenge

A practical, self-paced plan to build software-engineering interview skills over one year. The goal is consistent practice and clear evidence of progress—not a guarantee of an offer. Adapt the pace to your experience, target roles, and available time.

## Daily practice

Aim for 60–90 minutes on most days; take rest or catch-up days when needed.

1. **Learn (15–20 min):** Review one concept or a previous mistake.
2. **Practice (30–45 min):** Solve or explain one focused problem. Start with a straightforward approach, then discuss trade-offs and improve it.
3. **Communicate (10–15 min):** Explain your reasoning aloud and state complexity where relevant.
4. **Reflect (5 min):** Record what you learned, what was difficult, and what to revisit.

A missed day is not a failure. Resume with the next planned session rather than trying to cram.

## Roadmap

| Days | Focus | Suggested outcomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–30 | Foundations and baseline | Choose a programming language; refresh complexity, arrays, strings, hash maps, and basic problem-solving; complete a baseline mock and identify gaps. |
| 31–90 | Core data structures | Practice linked lists, stacks, queues, binary search, trees, heaps, and intervals; revisit problems you could not solve independently. |
| 91–150 | Advanced problem patterns | Study graphs, recursion, backtracking, dynamic programming, greedy methods, and common string techniques; explain when each approach applies. |
| 151–210 | Practical engineering | Review object-oriented design, APIs, databases, concurrency basics, testing, debugging, and the fundamentals of system design appropriate to your target level. |
| 211–270 | Communication and role preparation | Practice behavioral stories, project deep-dives, requirements clarification, trade-off discussions, and timed interview simulations. Research each target company using current, reliable information. |
| 271–330 | Mock interview cycle | Complete regular coding, design, and behavioral mocks; track recurring feedback and spend practice time on the highest-impact gaps. |
| 331–365 | Review and readiness | Revisit weak areas, polish concise project explanations, run realistic full interview loops, and prepare thoughtful questions for interviewers. |

This is a sequence of themes, not a required problem list. Choose exercises that fit your level and target role; prioritize understanding over volume.

## Weekly rhythm

- **Days 1–4:** Learn and practice one topic at a time.
- **Day 5:** Mix topics and solve without looking at notes.
- **Day 6:** Do a mock interview, design exercise, or behavioral practice.
- **Day 7:** Review mistakes, plan the next week, or rest.

## Progress log

Copy this template into your own notes for each session:

```text
Day / date:
Topic:
Exercise or prompt:
Approach and trade-offs:
What I got stuck on:
What I learned:
Next review date:
Confidence (1–5):
```

Track concepts understood, explanations practiced, and lessons applied—not just problems completed. Revisit a problem after a delay and try it again without reading the solution first.

## Interview practice principles

- Clarify requirements and edge cases before coding or designing.
- State assumptions, outline an approach, and invite feedback.
- Test representative cases and discuss complexity and trade-offs.
- For behavioral questions, use specific, truthful examples and explain your own contribution and learning.
- Use only materials you are allowed to use in an interview; do not share confidential interview questions or employer information.
- Keep the plan sustainable and adjust it for accessibility, work, school, and wellbeing.
